title: "Scan Network"
icon: "radar"
description: "Find devices on your network and add connections from what the gateway discovers."

Typing in the IP address, port, and protocol for every device on your floor is slow and error-prone — especially when you're not sure what's actually out there. **Scan Network** finds devices for you: it scans your network, tells you what ports are open on each device, and lets you turn any of them into a connection in a couple of clicks.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the scanner">
    From the connections list, click **Add Connection → Scan Network**. The modal immediately starts scanning your machine's local subnet and loopback address, checking each device for common protocol ports: OPC-UA, MQTT, Modbus, EtherNet/IP, Siemens S7, FOCAS, MTConnect, SQL Server, PostgreSQL, MySQL, InfluxDB, and web ports used by HTTP, GraphQL, and WebSocket.

    A progress bar and an address-scanned counter show how the scan is progressing.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add other networks to scan (optional)">
    The automatic scan only covers the subnet your gateway machine is directly attached to. If your devices live on a different subnet or VLAN — for example, a dedicated OT network reached through a router — enter its range in **CIDR notation** (e.g. `10.0.1.0/24`) and click **Add** to include it in the scan.

    As you type, a live preview shows exactly what will be scanned: the network address, subnet mask, the first and last host address actually reachable, and the total address count.

    <Note>
      To keep scans fast, any range looser than a **/24** is rejected — the **Add** button stays disabled and an error explains why. A /24 (254 usable addresses) is the largest single range you can add at once. If you need to cover a larger block, add it as multiple /24 (or smaller) ranges.
    </Note>

    <Accordion title="Not sure what a CIDR range like 10.0.1.0/24 means?">
      Hovering the range input shows an in-depth breakdown, but here's the short version:

      A CIDR range is written as `address/prefix`, where the prefix (`/24`) is the number of bits — out of 32 — reserved for the **network**. The remaining bits are **host** bits, and every combination of those host bits is one address on that network.

      For `/24`, that's 32 − 24 = 8 host bits, giving 2⁸ = 256 possible addresses. Two are reserved (the network address itself and the broadcast address), leaving **2⁸ − 2 = 254** usable host addresses — which is why `/24` is the scan limit.

      | Prefix | Host bits | Usable addresses |
      |--------|-----------|-------------------|
      | /24 | 8 | 254 |
      | /28 | 4 | 14 |
      | /30 | 2 | 2 |
    </Accordion>

    Ranges you add manually are saved to a **Recently used** list (stored on this device only, not synced to your account) so you don't have to retype them next time. Click an entry to re-add it, remove entries individually, or clear the list entirely.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Track scan progress">
    Each network being scanned appears as a pill showing its CIDR range and a running device count. Hover a pill to see its subnet breakdown: mask, address range, total address count, and how many devices have been found out of the total scanned so far.

    <Tip>
      If you add or change a network range after a scan has already finished, a **Refresh to update** prompt appears with a highlighted **Refresh** button — so you're never looking at results that are missing a network you just added.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review discovered devices">
    Results appear in a sortable table:

    | Column | Description |
    |--------|-------------|
    | **IP** | The device's address. |
    | **Network** | Which scanned CIDR range the device was found on. |
    | **MAC Address** | Resolved via ARP. |
    | **Vendor** | Looked up from the MAC address's OUI (organizationally unique identifier), when recognized. |
    | **Detected Ports** | Every open port found on the device, each matched against the protocols that commonly use it. |

    Use the filter controls at the far right of the table header to narrow results by IP, MAC address, port, network, vendor, or likely protocol — handy once a scan turns up dozens of devices.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a connection from a result">
    Click any detected port on a device to choose a protocol for it. Likely protocols for that port are listed first, so in most cases you just confirm the default. This opens the standard **Add Connection** form, pre-filled with that device's IP, port, and protocol — review the rest of the fields and save.

  </Step>
</Steps>
